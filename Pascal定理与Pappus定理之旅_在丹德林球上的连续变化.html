Eccentricity: e = cosγ / cosα▾
Cross-section through the cone axis, perpendicular to the directrix: the sphere appears as the inscribed circle, touching the section line ℓ at F and the generators at T. σ is the plane of the contact circle; D = ℓ ∩ σ is the trace of the directrix, and PK = PD for P in this section.
1. PF = PT — two tangent segments from P to the same sphere.
2. Drop the auxiliary PQ ⟂ σ. Two right triangles share PQ: PQ = PT·cosα (generator at angle α to the axis) and PQ = PD·cosγ (ℓ at angle γ to the axis).
3. Hence PT·cosα = PD·cosγ, so e = PF / PK = PT / PD = cosγ / cosα — constant for every P on the conic.
γ > α → e < 1 ellipse (γ = 90°: e = 0 circle) · γ = α → e = 1 parabola · γ < α → e > 1 hyperbola
Same section, both spheres: the axial gap Δ = O₁O₂ projects onto ℓ as F₁F₂ (×cosγ) and onto the generator as T₁T₂ (×cosα). With 2c = F₁F₂ and 2a = T₁T₂ = PF₁ + PF₂: c / a = cosγ / cosα = e.
Directrix equations▾
The directrix is π ∩ σ (cutting plane × contact plane), perpendicular to the focal axis. Its position follows from PF = e·PK evaluated at a vertex V — write K′ for V's own foot on the directrix (where the axis meets it), and use e = c/a from the panel above.
Ellipse: VF = a − c, so VK′ = (a−c)/e and OK′ = a + (a−c)/e = a/e = a²/c. Directrices: x = ±a²/c.
Hyperbola: VF = c − a, so VK′ = (c−a)/e and OK′ = a − (c−a)/e = a/e = a²/c — the directrix lies between O and V. Directrices: x = ±a²/c.
Parabola: e = 1 gives PF = PK everywhere; at the vertex VF = VK′ = p/2 (p = focus-to-directrix distance). With the vertex at the origin: directrix x = −p/2, matching y² = 2px.
Pascal & Pappus — how to operate▾
Pascal's theorem
For six points A B C a b c on a conic, the three intersections P₁ P₂ P₃ of the cross-joined lines Ab×aB, Bc×bC, Ca×cA are collinear (the white dashed line in the scene).
Pappus's theorem
As γ→0 the section degenerates into two generators through the apex (a broken angle); the six points split 3+3 onto the two lines, and the three intersections stay collinear — Pappus is the limiting case of Pascal.
Ratios in this figure
· At the fold vertex O: Ab∥aB ⟺ OA·Oa = OB·Ob; all three products equal ⟺ all three cross-joined pairs parallel (parallel Pappus — the intersections are collinear on the line at infinity).
· Circle section (γ = 90°): the six points form a regular inscribed hexagon; P₁A·P₁b = P₁B·P₁a (intersecting-chords theorem; on a general conic this becomes the power of a point with respect to the curve).
· Cross-ratio: projecting four points from any point of the conic gives a pencil of constant cross-ratio — the projective root of the P₁P₂P₃ collinearity.
How to operate
1. Press Pascal·Sym (gold when on): the six points A B C a b c, the six cross-joined lines, and the white dashed Pascal line through P₁ P₂ P₃ appear. You can drag any of the six points directly on the cone to reshape this mirror-symmetric configuration.
2. With Pascal·Sym on, press Pascal·Eq (pink when on): switch to the equal-product configuration OA·Oa = OB·Ob = OC·Oc — then drag γ down to 0 and the three cross-joined pairs become parallel (parallel Pappus). Press Pascal·Eq again to return to your own Sym configuration.
3. Drag the γ slider — or nudge with − / + — to morph the section: circle → ellipse → parabola → hyperbola → two lines. The gold 1°/0.1°/0.01° button sets the step. With Pascal·Sym on, the teal ◀ ⏸ ▶ buttons light up: automatic reverse-play / pause / forward-play of γ at the current step size, pausing by itself at 0° and 90°.
4. With Pascal·Sym off, ▶ Play (blue) lights up: it sends the green point P traveling around the section curve. The P slider sets its position, Speed its rate.
5. ↻ resets the camera and all parameters; 📊 (top right) toggles the live PF / PT readout.
